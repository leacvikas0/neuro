Multiplication is an area

3 × 4 isn’t “three times four”. It’s the rectangle that appears when you lay 3 rows of 4 side by side — 12 cells. He saw it immediately. Set the sliders and watch the rectangle do the arithmetic.

Decimals are cut lines

0.5 × 4 is half a row. The rectangle doesn’t shrink into fog — it gets cut. Fractions are just partial rectangles; the knife comes from the slider.

the open question

What does 3 × (−2) look like? You can’t draw “less than nothing” of a rectangle — not yet. He leaned in. Then the session pivoted to system design, and the question walked away, unanswered.

02

The question that walked away

First item on the journal’s list: resolve it — geometrically. Here is the picture the next session will find. The answer was hiding in the word “multiply” all along.

3 × 2 = 6  no flips — one side of the line drag the red handles — cross zero and the rectangle flips
  1. The rectangle lives against a line — zero. Above it: positive. Below: negative.
  2. −1 doesn’t shrink the rectangle. It flips it across the line. Same shape, other side.
  3. 3 × (−2): the 3-by-2 rectangle, flipped below the line. Six cells of it — exactly the same shape — but on the other side. That’s why we call it −6.
  4. Flip once and you cross (−). Flip twice and you’re home (+). So (−2) × (−3) flips twice: 6.
  5. This is why negatives exist in math at all: they’re the flips of the shape world.
the resolution — reserved

He gets to find this first

The journal lists it as the next move. This page resolves it for you, the reader — the lesson keeps the answer wrapped, waiting for the day he reaches across zero himself. When he does, the rectangle flips, and “negative number” stops being a rule and becomes a memory of crossing a line.

Watch the dashed ghost when you drag a handle below zero: it shows where the rectangle was, before the flip. The flip is the arithmetic.

Break it: what happens when both handles cross? What does a rectangle that flipped twice look like? Where does the sign land — and where does it feel like it should land?

Areas of areas — the next question

2 × 3 is a layer. Stack the layers and a new direction appears: 2 × 3 × 4 is a box of 24. Multiply a multiplication and you grow a dimension.

2 × 3 × 1 = 6 cells — a flat layer

Arrows — vectors

A vector is a walk: 3 right, 2 up. Walk one arrow after another and you’ve added them — head to tail, no formulas. Drag the heads; the dashed arrow is the landing point.

a + b = (4, 1)

Shadows — the dot product

Ask how much one arrow runs along another: drop its shadow. Shadow × length of the other = the dot product — one number for “how much same direction”. Perpendicular arrows cast no shadow.
